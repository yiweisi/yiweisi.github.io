My name is Yiwei Si. I hold a PhD in Linguistics from the [Faculty of Linguistics, Philology and Phonetics](https://www.ling-phil.ox.ac.uk/) at the [University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/), where I was supervised by [Dr Kerstin Hoge](https://www.mod-langs.ox.ac.uk/people/kerstin-hoge). My doctoral research was supported by the [Clarendon Scholarship](https://www.clarendonscholarsassociation.co.uk/) and the [Hanseatic Scholarship](https://www.toepfer-stiftung.de/en/what-we-do/hanseatic-scholarship-programme). As part of the Hanseatic Scholarship, I was a visiting researcher at the [Leibniz-Centre General Linguistics (ZAS)](https://www.leibniz-zas.de/de/) in Berlin from 2025 to 2026.My main research interest lies in morphology and its interfaces with phonology, syntax, and semantics. In my DPhil thesis, I explore how verbal particles in German contribute to the expression of aspect from both syntactic and semantic perspectives, using a neo-constructionist approach to word formation. I’m additionally interested in syntax, psycholinguistics and first language acquisition.Before starting my PhD, I completed an MPhil in Linguistics at Oxford in 2022. I earned my Bachelor’s degree in German Studies from Beijing Foreign Studies University in China.Outside of linguistics, I enjoy exploring local restaurants, practicing yoga, playing tennis, and travelling.
